02 — How it works

How the rotation is built

  1. It counts the playing time available. Players on the field × periods — a 7v7 game in quarters has 28 player-quarters to share.
  2. It splits that as evenly as possible. No player gets more than one period more than any teammate. With 10 kids in 7v7, eight play three quarters and two play two.
  3. It spreads the sits. Nobody sits a second time before a teammate who still owes a sit, and the sitting order moves around the roster.
  4. It rotates the keeper. In 7v7 and up, a different player takes goal each period where possible. Keeper time counts as playing time.

The kids at the top of your list get the extra period when time does not divide evenly. Use Shuffle who sits first, or reorder your list each week, so the same kids are not always the ones with less time. Working to an AYSO minimum? Read AYSO playing time rules, explained.

03 — At the field

Tips for running subs on game day

  • Print two copies — one for you, one for the assistant coach or team parent who calls the subs.
  • Line up the next group early. A minute before the break, gather the kids who are going in so the change takes seconds.
  • Tell the kids before kickoff. “You sit second quarter” ends the “am I in?” questions.
  • Mark changes in pen. If a kid arrives late or gets hurt, cross out and write the swap so your season record stays true.
04 — FAQ

Rotation template: common questions

How do I make a fair soccer substitution rotation?

Count your player-periods: players on the field × number of periods (7 × 4 = 28 in a 7v7 game of quarters). Divide by your roster to see how many periods each kid gets, then spread the sits so nobody sits twice before every teammate has sat once. The template on this page does that for you.

Can I print a blank rotation sheet to fill in by hand?

Yes. Choose “Blank grid” and the sheet prints with your players’ names and empty boxes for every period, plus columns for goals and notes — handy if you prefer to decide subs at the field.

Does the rotation template follow AYSO playing time rules?

It spreads time as evenly as the math allows and shows how many periods each player gets, so you can check it against your region’s minimum — half of every game nationally, three quarters in some regions. See our AYSO playing time guide for the details.

How many players should sit each quarter?

Roster size minus players on the field. Ten kids in a 7v7 game means three sit each quarter; twelve kids in a 9v9 game also means three.

Are my players’ names saved or uploaded?

No. The names stay in your browser on this device so the list is there next time; nothing is sent to SquadIQ or anyone else.

Can it rotate the goalkeeper too?

Yes. For 7v7, 9v9 and 11v11 the template gives a different player the keeper spot each period where possible, and keeper time counts as playing time. Check your region for limits on quarters in goal.
