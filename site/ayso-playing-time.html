01 — The national rule

Everyone Plays: at least half of every game

Short answer: AYSO requires every player on every team to play at least 50% of every game. Some regions raise that to three quarters — check your region’s coach handbook.

Everyone Plays® is the first of AYSO’s Six Philosophies. The official AYSO wiki puts it this way:

“AYSO’s goal is for kids and adults to play soccer, so we mandate that every player on every team must play at least 50 percent of every game.” — AYSO Wiki, Six Philosophies

AYSO’s own FAQ repeats it — “Every player on every team must play at least 50 percent of every game” — and adds the reason: “Spending the game on the bench is no way to learn soccer!” (ayso.org FAQ).

For a volunteer coach, that means three things:

  • It is per game, not per season. A kid who played the whole game last week still needs at least half this week.
  • It is a minimum, not a target. Most coaches aim for everyone to play as close to equal time as the roster allows.
  • Your region can add to it. National sets the floor; regions publish their own rules on top — including higher minimums and goalkeeper limits.
02 — Regional rules

Some regions require three quarters

A number of AYSO regions go beyond the national half-game minimum. Region 76 (Beverly Hills) is a clear, published example. Its Everyone Plays guidelines say each player “must play at least 3/4 of each game, except when that is mathematically impossible,” noting that AYSO National’s baseline is half.

Region 76’s guidelines also spell out what happens when the math does not work:

  • When it applies: all season for 6U–10U, and the regular season for 12U–14U.
  • 10U with 10 players: two players play half the game and eight play three quarters. The two who played half get three quarters in later games until every teammate has had a half-game turn.
  • 12U–14U with 15 players: one player is limited to half, then gets three quarters in later games until everyone else has had a turn.
  • Goalkeepers: 9U–10U players may play no more than two quarters in goal and must play at least one quarter in the field.

Source: AYSO Region 76 — Everyone Plays guidelines. Region 76’s coach handbooks state the same three-quarter commitment.

Your region may differ. Rules on the ¾ minimum, keeper limits, late arrivals and playoffs vary from region to region and can change between seasons. Your region’s coach handbook, or your division coordinator, is the final word.

03 — The math

How many players can meet the minimum?

Every game has a fixed number of player-periods to share: players on the field × number of periods. A 7v7 game in four quarters has 7 × 4 = 28. If each kid needs three quarters, 28 ÷ 3 means at most 9 players can all get there — a tenth kid makes it mathematically impossible, which is exactly the Region 76 10U exception above.

05 — On game day

Making a fair rotation work at the field

  • Plan before you arrive. Write the rotation at home and take it on a card — trying to work it out at a quarter break with ten kids asking “am I in?” is how mistakes happen.
  • Rotate who sits first. Nobody wants to always be the one who sits Q1. Start the sitting order from a different kid each game.
  • Spread the keeper job. Give different kids a turn in goal and check your region’s limit on quarters in goal.
  • Keep a season record. When the math forces someone down to half, write it down so they get more next week.
  • Use a printed sheet. Our free printable rotation template fills in names and prints a clean card for the clipboard.
06 — FAQ

AYSO playing time: common questions

How much does every player have to play in AYSO?

Under AYSO’s national Everyone Plays® philosophy, every player on every team must play at least 50 percent of every game — at least half. Some regions set a higher bar; AYSO Region 76, for example, requires three quarters of each game except when that is mathematically impossible.

Is the AYSO playing time rule half or three quarters?

The national minimum is half of every game. Three quarters is a regional rule: some regions adopt it in their own coach handbooks. Check your region’s coach handbook or ask your division coordinator which one applies to your team.

Does playing goalkeeper count as playing time?

Yes — a player in goal is on the field and playing. Regions often add separate limits on how many quarters one player may spend in goal (Region 76, for example, caps 9U–10U keepers at two quarters in goal), so check your region’s keeper rules too.

What if I have too many players to give everyone three quarters?

Sometimes the math does not work. With 10 players in a 7v7 game of four quarters there are only 28 player-quarters to share, so two kids can play just half. Region 76 handles this by having those two play three quarters in later games until everyone has had a turn at half. The calculator on this page shows when you have hit that limit.

Do players who arrive late still have to get their minimum?

Regional rules differ on late arrivals and players who are sick or injured. Your region’s coach handbook is the authority — when in doubt, give the late player as much time as you can and tell your referee or division coordinator.

How do I keep track of playing time across the whole season?

Write down who sat which period after every game, and give the kids who sat most last week the first spots this week. SquadIQ does this for you: it records each game’s lineup and builds the next one fairly from the season so far.
